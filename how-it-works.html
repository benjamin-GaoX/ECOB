HOW ECOB WORKS

More than a fast sizing formula.
A different way to find the optimum.

1

The traditional search checks every diameter.

Detailed hydraulic and cost models evaluate many candidate diameters — and repeat that search for every new scenario.

What if we could target the optimum directly?

2

A balance between steel and pumping.

Smaller pipes save steel but require more pumping. Larger pipes reduce hydraulic losses but increase investment.

3

A hidden pattern in the optima.

The search space is broad. The region of economic optima is much narrower.

3.1

Many possible diameters.

Every candidate changes the hydraulic and economic operating point.

3.2

Economic optima emerge.

The lowest-cost designs begin to stand out across different cases.

3.3

The optima cluster.

The preferred EVR states form a narrow, rising region rather than spreading across the entire search space.

3.4

This is the ECOB.

Target the economic operating region instead of searching every diameter one by one.

EVR = stream velocity / reference erosional velocityExplore the search space

4

How ECOB estimates the optimal diameter.

Three relationships behind a faster sizing estimate.

4.1

Predict ECOB.

ECOB captures the economically preferred operating state based on CO₂ flow conditions and project economics.

ECOB=f(m˙,Cel,Csteel,hop,CRF,fO&M,pipe)

4.2

From ECOB to optimal diameter.

Convert ECOB into an optimal inner diameter using a direct hydraulic relationship.

IDoptimal=4m˙πCρin·ECOB

4.3

Estimate the optimal diameter directly.

An extended correlation integrates flow conditions and project economics into a single sizing expression, without screening every candidate diameter.

IDoptimal=f(m˙,Cel,Csteel,hop,CRF,fO&M,pipe)

5

From a target diameter to a real pipeline.

The target inner diameter is mapped to a commercial NPS and wall-thickness schedule. The resulting pipe is then assessed for transport cost, pumping electricity, pumping stations and lifecycle GHG.

A simpler way to design CO₂ pipelines.
